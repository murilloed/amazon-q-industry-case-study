# Ethics, Privacy, and Licensing

The industrial archives are treated as restricted research material.

## Privacy

The codebase contains concepts related to personal identifiers and user
workflows. Even when no production record is present, data models, integration
logic, logs, and examples can reveal sensitive operational context. Public
release therefore follows data minimization rather than broad redaction.

## Confidentiality and Security

Deployment files, environment names, hostnames, credentials, authorization
rules, and external-system integrations are excluded from the public package.
Publishing placeholders after deleting only passwords would still disclose
unnecessary attack-surface information.

## Software Rights

Possession of an archive and removal of secrets do not establish the right to
redistribute institutional source code. Written authorization should identify:

- the software owner;
- permitted files and versions;
- applicable open-source license;
- treatment of contractor and third-party contributions; and
- restrictions on generated derivatives.

## Participant Evidence

Public qualitative evidence must be anonymized, consented for release, and
limited to what is necessary to support the findings. Raw transcripts and
identifiable review exports are not included by default.
